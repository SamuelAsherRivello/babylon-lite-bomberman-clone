- World and camera:
  - Create a single-screen, tile-based 2D arena inspired by SNES-era Super Bomberman battle mode.
  - Use a 15-column by 13-row grid with solid outer walls, indestructible pillars, destructible blocks, and clear starting areas in separate corners.
  - Use a fixed top-down orthographic camera showing the entire arena. Maintain a landscape play area with readable tiles on desktop and mobile.

- 2DPixelPerfect rendering:
  - Respect the template’s 2DPixelPerfect rendering policy and integration guidance. It is a template configuration, not a Babylon Lite API.
  - During the Foundation milestone’s OpenSpec exploration, inspect the current template’s rendering configuration, pixel-perfect helpers, and layout documentation:
    https://github.com/SamuelAsherRivello/github-repository-template/blob/HEAD/project-name/documentation/layout-and-game-integration.md
  - Adapt the relevant template improvements into this existing repository while preserving project-specific changes.
  - Choose and document the logical game resolution, tile size, and viewport aspect ratio that best suit the 15×13 arena, original pixel artwork, HUD, and desktop/mobile readability. Do not inherit the template’s 320×180 showcase resolution automatically.
  - Evaluate 16×16 logical-pixel tiles as a starting point: the arena alone would occupy 240×208 logical pixels. Choose the final stage dimensions after allowing sufficient space for HUD and controls and reviewing actual browser presentation.
  - Distinguish logical resolution, internal render resolution, canvas backing resolution, and CSS display size. Document their mapping and select an internal rendering approach that preserves authored pixel detail.
  - Use nearest-neighbor texture minification and magnification, disable mipmaps, use clamp-to-edge addressing, disable MSAA with msaaSamples: 1, and retain CSS image-rendering: pixelated.
  - Use centered integer logical-to-CSS scaling whenever the available space permits, with internal letterboxing instead of stretching.
  - When integer scaling cannot fit, use a positive fractional fit that keeps the entire arena visible. Document that strict pixel-alignment guarantees are suspended in this fallback.
  - Let Babylon Lite manage its DPR-aware canvas backing size. Apply DPR exactly once and avoid manually double-scaling canvas dimensions.

  - Record the chosen resolution and its rationale in the Foundation design and documentation. Revisit it during Gameplay Polish if browser playtesting reveals readability or scaling problems.

- Core loop:
  - Move through the arena, place timed bombs, destroy blocks, collect power-ups, and trap opponents while escaping explosions.
  - The last surviving player wins the round. The first player to win three rounds wins the match.

- Controls:
  - WASD or arrow keys to move and Space to place a bomb.
  - Provide an on-screen directional pad and bomb button for touch devices, supporting simultaneous movement and bomb placement.
  - Clear held inputs when the browser loses focus or touch input is cancelled.
  - Keep game controls available to the content layer; do not let React UI shortcuts intercept WASD, arrow keys, Space, or Enter.

- Competitive mechanics:
  - Create or join a room using a shareable room code, choose a distinct player color, and ready up together.
  - Start with 2–4 players and show a short countdown before each round.
  - Bomb explosions affect every player, including the player who placed the bomb.
  - Eliminated players spectate until the next round.
  - Resolve eliminations from the same server simulation tick together. Award no points when all remaining players are eliminated simultaneously.
  - Show round wins, player status, remaining time, and a rematch option after the match.

- Look and feel:
  - Create original pixel-art scenery, characters, bombs, blocks, power-ups, and explosion effects inspired by the readability and playful feel of SNES-era Super Bomberman.
  - Use crisp pixel rendering, a cohesive colorful palette, distinct player silhouettes and colors, expressive walking animations, and clear elimination feedback.
  - Make bomb fuses, explosion paths, solid walls, destructible blocks, and collectible power-ups easy to distinguish.
  - Create original arcade-style music and sound effects, with mute and volume settings.
  - Preserve the template’s corner UI roles: title upper left, project links upper right, version lower right, and settings lower left.
  - Keep important information readable without covering the arena or requiring scrolling to play.

- Gameplay requirements:
  - Players begin each round with one active bomb slot and an explosion range of two tiles.
  - Bombs explode after a 2.5-second fuse, producing a cross-shaped blast along the grid.
  - Indestructible walls stop blasts. Destructible blocks are destroyed and stop that blast from continuing beyond their tile.
  - Explosions trigger other bombs immediately, allowing chain reactions.
  - Blasts remain dangerous for 0.5 seconds and eliminate players who touch them.
  - A player may leave a newly placed bomb tile, but cannot move back through the bomb while it remains active.
  - Include power-ups for additional active bombs, increased blast range, and faster movement.
  - Cap active bomb capacity at five and blast range at eight tiles. Provide three speed upgrades, each increasing base movement speed by 15%.
  - Hide power-ups beneath some destructible blocks. Reveal them after the destroying blast clears, and allow later explosions to destroy exposed power-ups.
  - Keep spawn areas clear and give every starting position equal escape routes. Use rotationally symmetric block placement and power-up distribution for fairness.
  - Use a two-minute round timer.
  - During the final 30 seconds, introduce clearly telegraphed sudden-death walls in a deterministic inward pattern. Warn players one second before each affected tile closes.
  - If no single survivor remains when time expires, declare a draw.
  - Reset the arena and player upgrades between rounds. Provide short breaks showing scores and a rematch option after the match.
  - Include clear instructions, loading and connection states, and useful recovery messages.
  - Show a useful unsupported-WebGPU or initialization-error message instead of a blank canvas.
  - In online matches, opening settings or losing focus stops local input while the authoritative match continues. Do not pause the shared simulation for one player.
